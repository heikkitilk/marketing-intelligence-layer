Q3 paid review, notes from the offsite

- Direct ROAS formula we settled on: ((portals × LTV) − spend) / spend. Took three quarters of arguing to agree on it. The fight was never about the maths, it was about whether to use LTV or first-year revenue. LTV won because it stopped the team optimising for cheap, bad customers.
- Budget split for demand capture ended up 85% SEM, 10% affiliate, 5% emerging (Reddit, ChatGPT referrals, LinkedIn). The 5% is a deliberate hedge, not a bet — search demand is flat and CPCs are up ~20% YoY.
- Creative testing: we were running 4 variants per campaign and learning nothing. Dropped to 2 variants with a much bigger delta between them. Learning rate went up immediately. Small variations produce statistically noisy results at our volume.

Attribution stuff (still unresolved)

- Last-touch was under-crediting content by roughly 40% when we ran the holdout test against it. Every senior person's gut said content mattered more than the dashboard said. The gut was right.
- Ran a proper geo holdout for the first time. Expensive, slow, and the only number anyone actually believed. Worth doing twice a year, not monthly.

SEO / organic

- The pages that won were the ones that answered a question a salesperson gets asked on every call. Not the ones targeting the biggest keyword volume.
- We killed 300 thin blog posts and organic traffic went *up* over the following two quarters.

Team + org notes

- Grew the demand gen team from 3 to 12 over about 18 months. The single highest-leverage hire was a marketing ops person, hired 4th. Should have been hired 1st. Every campaign before that hire was slower than it needed to be.
- Mistake I'd undo: I put the paid team under the demand gen lead for org-chart tidiness. It made paid a service function instead of a strategy function and the channel stagnated for two quarters.
- Exec comms: I stopped presenting MQLs to the board and started presenting pipeline created and pipeline velocity. The conversation changed completely — from "is marketing busy" to "is marketing working".

Onboarding / activation

- Time to first value was 6 days. Got it to under 1 hour by removing three optional setup steps from the flow. Activation rate roughly doubled.
- Counter-intuitive: adding a mandatory step *increased* activation, because it forced the one action that predicted retention. Fewer steps isn't automatically better — fewer steps before the aha moment is what matters.
